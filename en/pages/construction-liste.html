    Army building

    Everything starts from a Crusade Primary Detachment, to which you then add further detachments.

    This is the only mandatory detachment. You put in it only what you want: nothing inside it is compulsory. It is simply capped, at a maximum of:

    • 1 High Command — your big boss (praetor, some heroes…);
    • 3 Command — the line officers (centurions, chaplains, librarian…);
    • 4 Troops — your core squads (Tactical, etc.);
    • 4 Transports — rhinos and other vehicles to move everyone around.

    Example: 1 praetor (High Command), 1 centurion (Command), 2 tactical squads (Troops) and 1 rhino (Transport).

    For all other models (dreadnoughts, terminators, tanks…), you need to unlock additional detachments. Your Primary Detachment is the key:

    • Each Command lets you take 1 Auxiliary Detachment (Armoured Fist, Tactical Support…). You can take the same one several times.
    • The "Officer of the Line (2)"Allows a number of Auxiliary Detachments equal to X to be selected for the Command Slot concerned, instead of only one. rule (the Centurion, for example) lets you select not 1 but 2 Auxiliary Detachments for the Command concerned.
    • If you have taken a High Command, you can select 1 Apex Detachment (Combat Escort, Officer Cadre, Army Vanguard) or 1 Auxiliary Detachment (like a Command).

    In short: the more officers you take in your Primary Detachment, the more detachments you unlock for the rest of your collection.

    Remember: an army must include 1 Primary Detachment. It can always include any number of Allied Detachments, up to 1 Lord of War Detachment, and a number of Auxiliary/Apex Detachments determined by your Command and High Command Slots.

    These three Advanced Reactions are available to any unit made up solely of models with the Knight sub-type, whatever its household paradigm — in addition to each household's own Advanced Reaction.

    Stomp

    This Reaction allows the Reactive Player to move a knight towards an enemy unit that has come within 8 inches and make a stomp attack.

    • Trigger: at step 2 of the Reposition Sub-Phase process, if an enemy unit made up solely of Infantry, Cavalry or Automata models ends a move within 8 inches of and in Line of Sight of a unit controlled by the Reactive Player made up solely of models with the Knight sub-type.
    • Cost: 1 Reaction Allotment, paid upon declaration.
    • Target: the Target Unit is always the unit whose move triggered the Reaction. Once the cost has been paid, the Reactive Player chooses a unit under their control within 8 inches of and in Line of Sight of the Target Unit, made up solely of models with the Knight sub-type: this unit is the Reacting Unit.

    Process

    1. Once the Stomp Reaction has been declared, the Active Player must fully resolve the Target Unit's move.
    2. Once the Target Unit has finished its move, the Reactive Player may move each model in the Reacting Unit up to a number of inches equal to its base Initiative characteristic, straight towards the Target Unit, without ending within 1 inch of any enemy model that is not part of the Target Unit.
    3. Once this move is finished, the Reactive Player may place a 3-inch Blast marker in base contact with a single model in the Reacting Unit, without fully or partially covering any model that is not part of the Target Unit with that marker.
    4. Once this Blast marker has been placed, the Target Unit suffers a number of automatic hits equal to the number of its models fully or partially covered by the marker. These hits are resolved with a Strength equal to the highest base Hull Points characteristic of a model in the Reacting Unit, an AP of 3 and a Damage of 2.

    Angle Shields

    This Reaction allows the Reactive Player to improve a knight's Invulnerable Save by +1 against a single Shooting Attack, but modifies its Invulnerable Save by −1 against all subsequent Shooting Attacks in that Player Turn.

    • Trigger: in the Shooting Phase, at step 3 of the Shooting Attack sequence of any Shooting Attack made by the Active Player against a unit made up solely of models with the Knight sub-type.
    • Cost: free.
    • Target: always the unit that was the target of the Shooting Attack that triggered the Reaction.

    Process

    1. All models in the Reacting Unit immediately improve by +1 the Invulnerable Save they have against Shooting Attacks made against their front arc, for the duration of the Shooting Attack that triggered the Reaction.
    2. The Active Player finishes resolving the original Shooting Attack that triggered the Reaction.
    3. Once this Shooting Attack has been resolved, all models in the Reacting Unit modify by −1 all the Invulnerable Saves they have against Shooting Attacks made against their front arc, for the rest of the Active Player's turn.

    Joust!

    This Reaction allows the Reacting Unit to charge an enemy unit that has declared a charge.

    • Trigger: at the end of step 3 of the charge process of any charge declared by the Active Player where the charging unit is within 12 inches of a unit controlled by the Reactive Player, made up solely of models with the Knight sub-type and that is not the target of the charge.
    • Cost: 1 Reaction Allotment, paid upon declaration.
    • Target: the Target Unit is always the unit whose charge triggered the Reaction. The Reacting Unit is always a unit controlled by the Reactive Player within 12 inches of at least one model in the Target Unit, made up solely of models with the Knight sub-type and that is not the target of the charge.

    Process

    1. Once this Reaction is declared, the Reactive Player makes a Charge Roll for the Reacting Unit.
    2. If the result is enough to bring a model in the Reacting Unit into base contact with a model in the Target Unit, a Charge Move is made and the units are locked in combat. No model in the Target Unit can claim any bonus from a special rule that requires a successful charge, while the models in the Reacting Unit gain the bonuses of any special rules that apply on a successful charge.
    3. If the result is not enough to bring any model in the Reacting Unit into base contact with a model in the Target Unit, no Charge Move is made and the players move on to step 4 of the charge process.

    All units in the Legio Titanicus army list are Core units: they represent the backbone of the Legio Titanicus, backed by a wide range of models. Upcoming Expanded units will be described in rules downloadable from the Warhammer Community website, in White Dwarf magazine, or elsewhere. Both categories may be freely used in any Horus Heresy battle, with no effect either on their availability in an army or detachment or on the rules for using them in battle.

    Units from the Legio Titanicus army list can be fielded in two ways:

    • By selecting a single Lord of War choice in a Lord of War Detachment of an army that fills its Primary Detachment from another army list;
    • By using the Titanic Ordinal Primary Detachment, restricted to Engine Kill missions.

    The Legio Titanicus army list can also be used to fill an Allied Detachment, but as soon as an Allied Detachment includes a Lord of War unit, the points restrictions confine the use of such a list to battles that set a very high points limit.

    This Primary Detachment can only be used if the battle follows one of the Engine Kill missions, or another mission that allows it. A player cannot use this Primary Detachment when selecting an army for a Core mission.

    When using this Primary Detachment, ignore the limits on the amount or percentage of army points to be spent on Lords of War in all of the army's detachments; a player is allowed to spend all the points available for selecting an army that includes this Primary Detachment on a single Lord of War choice!

    The Titanic Ordinal comprises 1 Lord of War Slot, 1 Command Slot, 4 Troops Slots and 4 Heavy Transports Slots, all restricted to the Legio Titanicus Faction Trait.

    • An army using this Primary Detachment may also include a Lord of War Detachment, but that detachment cannot then include any unit from the Legio Titanicus army list.
    • Only units with the Legio Titanicus Faction Trait can fill the slots of this Primary Detachment. If a player wants to include units of a different Faction in this army, they must take an Allied Detachment of that Faction.

    As in other army lists, the units in the Legio Titanicus army list all have an allegiance, represented by a Trait in their unit profile. For units that are not tied to an allegiance, this is shown as "[Allegiance]". When such a unit is added to an army, this Trait is replaced by "Loyalist" or "Traitor", according to the allegiance chosen for the army — the same mechanism as for [Legiones Astartes].

    The rulebook describes the Titan Legions as they were before the schism of the Horus Heresy, the corruption of the Dark Mechanicum and Horus's civil war. This does not mean that only loyalist armies can be represented: within every forge world, factions of all sizes defied their High Princeps and the forge worlds to which they were vassals, to side with whichever camp seemed to them the rightful destiny of the Imperium. This is why players are free to use this book to create armies of either allegiance.

    Designer's note. Due to the extreme power of the Lords of War available in the Legio Titanicus army list, it is recommended that these units only be used in battles following the Engine Kill missions. Titans can be included in other missions under the normal rules for selecting Lords of War, or, in very large battles, through an Allied Detachment, but a Titan will most likely dominate the battle, leaving few tactical options to the opponent. In general, if a player wishes to field a Titan outside the Engine Kill missions, it is recommended to warn the opposing player well in advance so they can plan an army able to face such a war engine.

    Everything starts from a Crusade Primary Detachment, to which you then add further detachments.

    This is the only mandatory detachment. You put in it only what you want: nothing inside it is compulsory. It is simply capped, at a maximum of:

    • 1 High Command — your big boss (no Unit with this Battlefield Role is transcribed for this Faction yet);
    • 3 Command — the line officers (Skitarii Combat Peregrine Marshal);
    • 4 Troops — your core squads (Skitarii Combat Peregrine Corpus);
    • 4 Transports — the light vehicles to move your troops (no Unit with this Battlefield Role is transcribed for this Faction yet).

    Example: 1 Skitarii Combat Peregrine Marshal (Command) and 2 Skitarii Combat Peregrine Corpus (Troops).

    For all other models (dreadnoughts, terminators, tanks…), you need to unlock additional detachments. Your Primary Detachment is the key:

    • Each Command lets you take 1 Auxiliary Detachment (Armoured Fist, Tactical Support…). You can take the same one several times.
    • The "Officer of the Line (2)"Allows a number of Auxiliary Detachments equal to X to be selected for the Command Slot concerned, instead of only one. rule (a Model that has it, for example) lets you select not 1 but 2 Auxiliary Detachments for the Command concerned.
    • If you have taken a High Command, you can select 1 Apex Detachment (Combat Escort, Officer Cadre, Army Vanguard) or 1 Auxiliary Detachment (like a Command).

    In short: the more officers you take in your Primary Detachment, the more detachments you unlock for the rest of your collection.
